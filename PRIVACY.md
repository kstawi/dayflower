# Dayflower privacy policy

Effective 6 November 2026.

Dayflower is a calendar app that runs on your Windows PC. It has no servers of its own: the developer never
receives, stores or sees your data.

## What Dayflower accesses

When you add a Google account, Google asks you to let Dayflower:

- read and change your Google Calendar data: your calendars, events, invitations, guests, reminders and the
  free/busy times of people you invite, so Dayflower can show your calendar and make the changes you ask for;
- see your account's email address, so Dayflower can tell your accounts apart.

Dayflower only uses this data to show and manage your calendar on your PC.

## Where your data goes

- **To Google only.** Dayflower talks directly from your PC to Google's APIs. It sends nothing to the developer
  or to anyone else.
- **Kept on your PC.** Calendar data is cached in Dayflower's folder in your Windows user profile, so the app
  works offline. Google sign-in tokens are encrypted with Windows (DPAPI) for your Windows user account.
- **Meeting links** open in your default web browser when you click Join.
- **No analytics, telemetry or ads.** Dayflower doesn't track how you use it. Its log files stay on your PC.
- Dayflower doesn't sell, rent or share your data.

Dayflower's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the
[Google API Services User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy),
including the Limited Use requirements.

## Removing your data

- Removing an account in Dayflower deletes that account's sign-in tokens and cached calendar data from your PC.
- Uninstalling Dayflower deletes all of its data from your PC.
- You can withdraw Dayflower's access to your Google account at any time at
  <https://myaccount.google.com/permissions>.

## Changes
